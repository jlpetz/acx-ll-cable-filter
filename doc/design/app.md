# App spec (nf-app → `noise-filter.exe`, v0)

## Start-up (main.rs)
- **The CPU gate, first:** the build is x86-64-v3 (`.cargo/config.toml`). `main` calls
  `cpu_check::gate` before anything else (the panic hook, the log, the console, the single-instance
  check, the memory pool), and the rest runs in `app_main`, which is never inlined into it. A CPU
  that lacks a v3 feature (CPUID and XCR0, read directly) gets an ASCII `MessageBoxA` naming what's
  missing, the same text on standard error if there is one, and exit code 5. The launcher does the
  same on standard error only, and in a console of its own waits for Enter before it exits.
  `cpu_check.rs` says why the way there avoids AVX and BMI instructions;
  `tools/check_cpu_gate_asm.py` checks that in the release asm (`doc/dev/build-report.md` §11).
- **Allocator:** `#[global_allocator] static GLOBAL: nf_mem::RtAllocator = nf_mem::RtAllocator::new();`
- **Memory, in this order, before any engine thread exists:**
  1. `nf_mem::privilege::enable_lock_memory_privilege()`; a missing privilege isn't an error;
  2. `nf_mem::install_pool(PagePolicy::Large, 32 MiB)`;
  3. `nf_mem::set_working_set_minimum(64 MiB)`.
  Log the `PoolStatus`. The engine reads `nf_mem::pool_status()` for its status line.
- **Logging:** a small `log::Log` that appends to `%LOCALAPPDATA%\noise-filter\noise-filter.log`
  (with a 1 MB rotation to `.old`). Only non-real-time threads log. Add a panic hook that logs the
  panic and its location. Release is `panic = "abort"`.
- **Window or console:** a double-click opens only the window, never a console. CLI subcommands
  from a terminal print to it. Choose the mechanism from `doc/dev/api/windows-reactor.md`:
  - TMR's console subsystem plus the `consoleAllocationPolicy` manifest, which works on 26100+;
  - or the windows subsystem plus `AttachConsole(ATTACH_PARENT_PROCESS)` for subcommands.

  Windows 11 22000-26099 must not flash a console on a double-click. Document the choice in
  `main.rs`. The choice made: the windows subsystem plus `AttachConsole(ATTACH_PARENT_PROCESS)`.
- **The console launcher:** cmd.exe and PowerShell don't wait for a windows-subsystem exe, so
  `noise-filter-cli.exe` (console subsystem, about 140 KB) sits next to it. It ignores Ctrl+C
  itself, runs `noise-filter.exe` with the same arguments and inheritable copies of its standard
  handles, waits, and exits with the child's code; the child attaches to the console and handles
  Ctrl+C. Copied to `noise-filter.com`, a plain `noise-filter ...` in a terminal runs it (PATHEXT
  puts .COM first). Nothing creates the `.com` yet, and Win+R or Start search would then pick it
  too and flash a console: the packaging decides (TODO #18). Without the launcher, `run` warns
  once that Ctrl+C may not reach it.
- **One window:** a second window launch finds the first (a named mutex, then its visible window)
  and brings it forward instead of opening another. The headless `run` isn't guarded: both can
  feed the cable.

## Commands
| Command | What |
|---|---|
| *(none)*, `gui` | Opens the window: `nf_gui::run(Arc::new(Engine::new()), settings_path)`. |
| `devices` | Lists capture and render endpoints: name, id, default marks, mix format. |
| `devices --check [--seconds N] [run's options]` | Device health (TODO 21): the engine runs as `run` would on the chosen mic, output and reference (the same defaults; echo cancelling on unless `--no-aec`, so the speakers' loopback is checked too; the latency readout forced on, as its budget times the mic's delivery) for `--seconds` (default 15, 5-120), with the chain recorder armed before the start (its per-block facts, about 0.6 MB a second), then prints a report per stream (`health.rs`, pure and tested; `commands/check.rs`): a verdict (steady, or in bursts / late / slow to play / drifting / glitched), the figures it rests on, and what to try. The figures are the run's it began with: the streams as it opened them, the blocks of its chain recording, and its counters as its last once-a-second publication had them (the check waits for one more after its time is up, so its last moments count); after a restart, the first run's (its counters up to its last whole second), with a warning. **Mic** (from the run's second second on, as the output): the share of blocks that came two or more to a wake, the gaps between delivering wakes, a block's age when read (its delivery: 5th-95th percentile, the oldest; what the bursts add, mean less the 5th percentile), mic glitches and stalls, a refused exclusive mode and why. A mic without trusted stamps is judged by its wakes alone (bunched blocks, or wake gaps 3 ms over a block's 10 ms at their 95th percentile, are bursts) and otherwise reads `steady by its wakes (not timed)`, never plain steady; one that delivered nothing after the first second reads `delivered nothing`, with the privacy setting and the device's mute among what to try. A stream the engine didn't describe gets no verdict that needs its period. **Output:** its events (underruns and the silence an exclusive device played, deficits, coarse drops, slips, re-anchors), the render side's mean (how long the device and its driver hold the audio after taking it) and the queue against its target. **Echo reference:** after its start-up (250 blocks and 10 more from its alignment), the share of blocks the loopback was within two periods or short of samples, its lead and target, the start-up's raise of its target (with the exclusive mic up to a loopback period of it is that mic's own need, and said) and any raise after it, whether the stamps timed it and how far the readings scatter, slips and jumps. **Each clock's drift** against the mic's, fitted over the blocks after the first second (`nf_engine::clock_drift`: the output device's clock readings, `OutputFacts::clock_pos`, and the loopback's timeline against its stamped tap times, each against the mic's index and capture stamps, or its delivery without them; least squares with an intercept a stretch between glitches), not the drift loops' estimates, which take a minute or more to settle and which bursts throw hundreds of ppm off meanwhile. It prints as fast or slow with its 95% interval and the span fitted; a fit looser than ±50 ppm (a mic timed by its delivery, a short check) gives no verdict. What to try: another USB port, the maker's driver, audio enhancements off, LatencyMon for DPC latency, exclusive mode (or turning it off, for glitches), `--no-adaptive-output`; a virtual cable is told that it delivers in bursts by design. **The thresholds are provisional**, set from the dev VM's cables (bursty: half the blocks two to a wake, a block 10-21 ms old; steady: a block a wake, about 10.7 ms old): 5% bunched or a 3 ms swing (p95 less median) is bursty, a mean 3 ms past the packet late, a loopback stale at 2% of blocks late and owed at 1% (or its target raised 3 ms after its start-up, or in it 3 ms past the exclusive mic's need) bursty, a render side 10 ms past half its period slow, 300 ppm drifting (the printed, rounded figure); real hardware's figures are to set them (TODO 21's detail file). Nothing is saved (no calibration yet). Exit 0 once it has run; 3 when Ctrl+C stopped it in its first 3 s; 2 when the filter couldn't start. |
| `run [--seconds N] [--config FILE] [--mic ID] [--output ID] [--reference ID] [--aec] [--no-ns] [--floor DB] [--gate off\|static\|auto] [--threshold DB] [--no-exclusive-output] [--no-exclusive-mic] [--no-adaptive-output]` | A headless engine. `--seconds N` (1-86400) stops it after N seconds (a soak); Ctrl+C or the console closing stops it either way. It ends with a **summary** of the whole run (a `Tally` of the status lines' snapshots, across the engine's own restarts): its length and the seconds it ran, the dropouts (mic glitches and stalls, output underruns, reference gaps and jumps, and those after the first 5 s, past the reference's start-up), the output's events (`OutputEvents`: underruns and the silence an exclusive device played, deficits and their zeros, coarse drops and the samples they took, slips and their net, re-anchors), the readout (the seconds' medians: their median and range; the seconds' p95s: their median and the worst second, with when), the budget's means over the run and the median second's p95s, the drift of each loop at the end, the reference's lead and target, the output target, the seconds the input clipped and the real-time heap allocations, and a result line: clean, or what wasn't. The counters are published once a second, so after the loop it waits for one more publication (up to 1.5 s) and counts it: an event in the final second isn't lost. With `--seconds` (a soak) its exit code says whether it was clean: 6 for mic glitches or stalls, output underruns or coarse drops, a fault that stopped the filter (seconds not running, a restart), or any real-time heap allocation (the real-time rule broken); else 3 when Ctrl+C cut it short; else 0. A run without `--seconds` is someone using the filter, not a test: it exits 0 whatever its summary says. Reference gaps and jumps are in the summary but not the code (the start-up has a few by design, and they touch the canceller's reference, not the audio). Prints a status line once a second (state, input/output peak/RMS dBFS, `CLIP` when the input clipped since the last line (the clip latch, taken by each line: TODO 12) or `near-clip`, latency median/p95 and its budget's means `[mic delivery+wait+output queue+render side]` and, with the adaptive target, `target` and the output's target now; CPU p99, drift, dropouts, `excl out+mic` (or `out`, `mic`) for the streams in WASAPI exclusive mode, RT allocations). The latency options (engine.md, "Exclusive mode" and "The output target") are **on by default** (the owner, 2026-11-02: the lowest latency by default, while some will turn them off, e.g. to record the raw mic; the output cable's recording side, which the apps read, stays shared either way). `--no-...` turns one off; the plain flag (`--exclusive-output`, `--exclusive-mic`, `--adaptive-output`) turns it back on over a `--config` that has it off; the last of a pair wins. Exclusive output plays into the device's driver at its smallest period (2 ms on VB-CABLE; nothing else can play into it meanwhile, fine for the output cable; never while it is Windows' default playback device, where no other app could play sound: that runs shared, with its warning saying so); the exclusive mic keeps every other app from recording that mic meanwhile (they use the filtered mic; its buffer is one period, 3.3 ms on VB-CABLE against shared mode's 40, so a filter thread held up longer loses mic audio, a mic glitch; and with AEC it lengthens the reference's lead, so the canceller needs the echo 20 ms or more after the speakers' mix, 14 shared). A device that refuses runs shared, with a **note** (not a warning: a refusal is ordinary now) saying which stream, why, what it costs, how to fix it and where to turn it off (`note:` lines). With both exclusive by default, a `run`, `latency` or `quality` on the window's mic or cable can't start while the window's filter runs (the device is held in exclusive mode: the start error says another copy of the filter may hold it, and `run` retries): stop the window's filter first. The adaptive target walks down after two minutes to what the delivery needs (an underrun under the fixed target puts it back and holds it there for 10 minutes, then 20, and the third keeps it fixed: up to three glitches a run that the fixed target wouldn't have). `--help` says what each costs and when to turn it off. The start line names each stream's asked-for mode and the target's (`exclusive output, exclusive mic, adaptive output target`), the stream lines the format of an exclusive stream (`exclusive 16-bit`). Ctrl+C stops it cleanly (`SetConsoleCtrlHandler`). Device IDs accept a name substring too (first match, case-insensitive). |
| `latency --probe DEV --capture DEV [--mode virtual\|acoustic] [--reps N] [--settle] [run's options]` | Starts the engine with those options (the product's defaults unless told otherwise), runs the latency test, prints the report (with the readout's budget over the sweeps: engine.md, "The latency budget"; and the output's events over them, `LatencyReport::output`: a spread between sweeps that they don't explain is the devices' own, TODO 9), and exits with 0 if `valid > 0`. Its conditions name each stream's mode (an exclusive one with its format, a refused one with why) and the output target: fixed, or adaptive and either "down from" it or "at the fixed target", a snapshot at the test's end. It measures at once, so the adaptive target is at its fixed start (it can come down only after 120 s of running), and says so before the sweeps; `--settle` waits for the target to settle first (to come down, walking for up to a minute, and hold still for 3 s; up to 188 s, saying so). `--settle` with `--no-adaptive-output` is a usage error (with a config that fixes the target, it says there's nothing to wait for); `--cable-only` refuses it. |
| `latency --cable-only --probe DEV --capture DEV [--reps N]` | No engine: the same sweeps from the probe straight to the capture (`nf_engine::cable_latency_test`, blocking this thread), timing one cable alone (probe play-out by its AudioClock to the arrival by the capture's QPC stamps). Prints a report labelled "cable only"; exit 0 if `valid > 0`. `run`'s options, `--mode` and `--through-model` are refused rather than ignored, so nobody reads the result as the filter's. |

## Exit codes
0 is OK; 1 is a usage error; 2 is an engine, device or file error; 3 is a test with no valid result
(`latency`: no valid repetition; `quality`: the recording didn't line up with the scene; either one
cancelled; `run --seconds` cut short by Ctrl+C with no glitch; `devices --check` cancelled in its first 3 s);
4 is the large-page helper started without elevation. `nf_mem::privilege::HELPER_NOT_ELEVATED`
holds the 4 the window reads, and a compile-time check ties it to `Exit::NotElevated`. 5 is a CPU
without x86-64-v3's features (`Exit::UnsupportedCpu`, tied by a compile-time check to
`cpu_check::EXIT_CODE`, which the CPU gate returns in both exes). 6 is `run --seconds` (a soak) that
wasn't clean (`Exit::Glitched`): mic glitches or stalls, output underruns or coarse drops, a fault that
stopped it (a second not running, a restart), or a real-time heap allocation, so a soak reads as one code;
it takes precedence over 3. The summary before it says what. A `run` without `--seconds` exits 0 (ours,
not the owner's: an interactive run stopped with Ctrl+C isn't a test, and the adaptive target allows up
to three underruns a run by design).
